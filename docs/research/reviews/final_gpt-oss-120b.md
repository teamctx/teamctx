**1. B1 – Epistemic contradiction / constructive definition of `deps_G`**  
**Resolution:** **Resolved**.  
The addendum makes the three‑valued valuation `⟦·⟧` a *meta‑theoretic* oracle that never appears in the broker’s runtime output.  By explicitly separating the broker’s public artifact `⟨C,κ⟩` from the abstract semantics used only in the soundness proof, the authors eliminate the value‑channel leak that would have arisen when a hidden source caused a transition `False → Unknown`.  Moreover, they give a concrete, conservative construction for `deps_G` (typed‑reference reachability plus policy‑mandated sources) and state it as a trusted‑rule obligation.  The added “sound consumer rule” and its accompanying lemma guarantee that any runtime decision made by a downstream consumer is a safe under‑approximation of the oracle semantics.  This fully addresses the reviewer’s concern that the original definition was non‑constructive and that the broker could inadvertently signal the existence of invisible artifacts.

**2. B2 – Quantitative leakage claim**  
**Resolution:** **Resolved**.  
The revised Theorem 6 now frames the leakage bound as a *single‑shot mutual‑information* statement with a clearly defined adversary model (one request, no timing or cardinality side‑channels).  The three dial settings (`δ=none`, `δ=count`, `δ=identity`) are each mapped to a tight upper bound (`0`, `log₂(M+1)`, `M` bits respectively) using the data‑processing inequality.  The authors also explicitly delimit the scope of the result, acknowledging that adaptive multi‑query leakage and other side‑channels remain open problems.  This satisfies the reviewer’s demand for an information‑theoretic formulation rather than a vague “≤log₂(N+1)” claim.

**4. Final Verdict**  
**Accept‑with‑nits** (i.e., accept pending the minor clarifications listed above).  

The authors have successfully addressed the two substantive blockers that previously prevented acceptance.
